# Rung 1 — Writing that is engineered to be read

Readability has two halves, and this file covers only one of them: **clarity** (here, via ASD-STE100)
and **conciseness** ([conciseness.md](conciseness.md)). They fail independently — a perfectly clear
document is still unreadable if there is three times too much of it. Apply both, and cut before you
polish.

## What ASD-STE100 is

**ASD Simplified Technical English** is a controlled natural language, originally written for
aerospace maintenance documentation (it grew out of AECMA/AIA work on ATA-100), now maintained by
the ASD and published as **ASD-STE100**. It has two parts: a set of **writing rules** (53 rules in
Issue 9) and a **controlled dictionary** — roughly 900 approved words, plus about 1,200 words to
avoid — where each approved word has exactly one meaning and one part of speech.

It is free to download from the ASD (<https://www.asd-ste100.org/>); the specification itself is
copyright ASD. You do not need to ship it. The reason it works as a prompt is that the model has
already read it, plus every manual written under it.

**Why it produces readable text:** the readability is not a style. It is the *output of
constraints*. Rules cannot be argued with, and they remove the reader's work — no synonym
decoding, no reaching back three clauses for the subject, no guessing whether the author meant a
requirement or a suggestion.

## The rules that carry most of the value

Use this list to steer, and to check your own output. It is a practical subset, not the spec.

| # | Rule | Instead of | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One word = one meaning. Never use two words for the same thing. | "begin", then "commence", then "initiate" | "start" every time |
| 2 | One meaning = one word. Do not reuse a word for a new sense. | "the job runs" / "the job of the pump" | "task" for the work |
| 3 | Active voice, simple tenses. | "The report is generated by the service" | "The service generates the report" |
| 4 | Short sentences: ≤ 20 words for instructions, ≤ 25 for description. | a 60-word sentence with three "and"s | three sentences |
| 5 | One instruction per sentence. | "Open the file and then check the header" | "Open the file." / "Check the header." |
| 6 | One topic per paragraph, ≤ 6 sentences. | a page-long block | short blocks with a lead sentence |
| 7 | Noun clusters ≤ 3 words. | "fuel pump inlet valve assembly" | "the valve at the inlet of the fuel pump" |
| 8 | No nominalizations. | "perform an inspection of the log" | "inspect the log" |
| 9 | Keep articles. Do not drop "the" or "a". | "Check connection point" | "Check the connection point" |
| 10 | No idioms, slang, metaphor, or humor. | "the queue is a bottleneck" | "the queue limits the throughput" |
| 11 | Technical nouns and verbs are allowed, but must be defined or listed. | undefined internal jargon | define on first use |
| 12 | Procedures use vertical lists, one action per step, imperative. | prose steps | numbered steps |
| 13 | Do not use one word as two parts of speech. | "oil the part" (noun → verb) | "apply oil to the part" |
| 14 | Say what the reader must do, not what they must understand. | "It is important to note that…" | the fact, stated |

## The dial: "80% of the way to ASD-STE100"

Full STE reads like a maintenance manual. That is exactly right for procedures, contracts, runbooks,
and API references, and exactly wrong for an explanation that needs a metaphor to land.

So treat compliance as a continuous knob, state the setting, and hold it:

| Setting | Keeps | Drops | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **100% STE** | all rules, approved vocabulary | metaphor, warmth, personality | procedures, runbooks, warnings, contracts, normative text |
| **~80% (default for explanations)** | rules 1–9 and 12–13 — the discipline | the approved-word list (normal vocabulary is fine), and **rule 10 is relaxed for exactly one clearly marked analogy** | explanations, design rationale, onboarding a colleague |
| **~50% (plain language)** | short sentences, active voice, one idea per sentence | the one-word-per-meaning lock, defined jargon | blog posts, READMEs, casual technical writing |

Rules for the dial:

- **Declare it.** "ASD-STE100 at about 80% — keep the discipline, allow one analogy."
- **Never let the dialect drift.** If the dial is at 100%, a single "commence" breaks the contract
  with the reader.
- **Mark the analogy.** At ~80%, allow metaphor only in an explicitly flagged sentence ("Think of
  it as a queue with a bouncer."). Everything else stays literal.
- **The dial applies per artifact, not per paragraph.** Mixed density is what makes text feel sloppy.

## Other controlled registers worth naming

Naming a register is the cheap, high-fidelity move. Pick the one the reader already expects:

- **RFC 2119 keywords** — MUST / SHOULD / MAY, for normative behaviour.
- **Google or Microsoft developer style** — for API docs and reference pages.
- **Plain language / Plain Writing Act style** — for public-facing or non-expert text.
- **Feynman / ELI5** — for intuition-first explanation, when precision is not the point.
- **中文技术写作规范 / 简化技术中文** — apply the same discipline to Chinese: 一句一事, 主动语态,
  一词一义, 术语首次出现即定义.
- **Socratic / FAQ form** — when the reader's real obstacle is a wrong prior belief, answering
  questions beats describing a system.

## Prompt shapes that work

- "Explain X in ASD-STE100. Use the writing rules and approved vocabulary of the spec."
- "Explain X at about **80% of the way to ASD-STE100**: keep the short sentences, active voice and
  one-word-one-meaning discipline, but normal vocabulary and one clearly marked analogy."
- "Rewrite the paragraph above to 100% ASD-STE100. Do not add or remove information."
- "Give me the version as a numbered procedure, one action per step, imperative form."
- "Now audit your own text against rules 1–9 and list every violation you find."

## When text is the right answer

Do not climb the ladder out of text when the reader needs:

- exact wording they will quote, copy, or comply with;
- something greppable, diffable, or reviewable in a PR;
- normative force (MUST / SHALL / MAY);
- density — a page of text can hold more facts than a minute of video, and the reader can skim it
  non-linearly;
- to paste it somewhere else.

Text is also the only rung that is trivially patchable. If the content will change tomorrow,
rungs 3 and 4 are the wrong investment.
